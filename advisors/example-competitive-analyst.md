## Key Research Findings

### The <TU_MERCADO> SaaS Opportunity (2025-2026)

> Section example for LATAM. Adapt with your region's data.

1. **Market size:** LATAM SaaS market valued at $22B in 2023, projected to reach $46B by 2027 (EBANX). Growth ~23% annually — fastest globally.
2. **SMB software penetration:** Less than 10% of SMBs in Brazil, Mexico, and Colombia use any software platform today. Most use Excel, paper, WhatsApp.
3. **First-time adoption drives growth:** LATAM growth comes primarily from first-time digital adoption (low switching costs, high education costs).
4. **75% of LATAM startups fail within 2 years** — one of the highest failure rates globally.
5. **SMB churn globally:** 3-7% monthly churn (31-58% annually). 43% of all SMB losses occur in the first 90 days.

## Competitor Profiles (LATAM example template)

### 1. Alegra (Colombia) — The Regional Champion

- **Website:** alegra.com
- **Founded:** 2012, Medellín
- **Funding:** $22.1M+ (Series A, Riverwood Capital, Endeavor Catalyst)
- **Employees:** ~696 across 5 continents
- **Users:** 365K+ in 25 countries
- **Partnership:** Mastercard, 100K+ SMEs across LATAM

**Pricing (Colombia, COP/mo):** Emprendedor $69,900 (~$17) / Pyme $149,900 (~$36) / Pro $219,900 (~$52) / Plus $279,900 (~$67). Also invoicing-only from $17,900 COP (~$4/mo).

**Core features:** E-invoicing (DIAN compliant), accounting, bank reconciliation, inventory, purchase orders, multi-currency, Shopify/Zapier.

**Strengths:**
- Largest Spanish-language cloud accounting in LATAM
- Deep regulatory compliance (DIAN, SUNAT, SAT)
- Mastercard distribution
- 25-country presence

**Weaknesses:**
- Zero analytics beyond basic reports
- No customer intelligence (no RFM, churn, segmentation)
- No forecasting or predictive capabilities
- Accounting-first DNA — thinks debits/credits, not insights
- No restaurant/vertical-specific modules
- POS is separate product

**Threat level: MEDIUM.** Owns accounting, doesn't compete on analytics. Risk: could add analytics features. Mitigation: depth would take them 12-18 months to replicate.

### 2. Siigo (Colombia) — The Acquisition Machine

- **Founded:** 1988, Bogotá
- **Funding:** $121M from Accel-KKR (PE)
- **Employees:** 2,700
- **Users:** 1.2M+ (including Aspel Mexico)
- **Presence:** Colombia, Mexico, Ecuador, Peru, Uruguay, Chile

**Key acquisitions:** Aspel (Mexico, 1M+ users, 39 years history), Ilimitada (Colombia), Kame ERP.

**Threat level: LOW-MEDIUM.** Plays in accounting/ERP, not analytics. PE ownership focuses on profitability, not innovation. Risk: could acquire an analytics company.

### 3. Treinta (Colombia) — The VC-Funded Pivot Machine

- **Founded:** 2020, Bogotá (YC W21)
- **Funding:** $60.8M total (32 investors including YC, Luxor Capital, PayU)
- **Users:** 7M+ across US and LATAM
- **2025 pivot:** Launched "idealane," AI app builder for non-tech entrepreneurs

**The pivot is telling.** After $60.8M raised and 7M users on a free bookkeeping app, pivoting to AI app building strongly suggests they could not monetize the SMB base effectively. **Classic LATAM trap: massive free user base, near-zero willingness to pay.**

**Key lesson:** Free-tier-driven growth in LATAM SMB ≠ monetizable customers. 7M users at $0 ARPU < 20 paying customers at $19-149/mo. Focus on willingness to pay from day one.

**Threat level: LOW.** Pivoting away from your space.

### 4. Bling (Brazil) — E-commerce ERP

- **Revenue:** $35M ARR (May 2025)
- **Employees:** 439
- **Users:** 1M+
- **Market:** Brazil (95%+)

**Pricing:** R$55-650/mo (~$10-120), 5 users to 80 users.

**Strengths:** E-commerce marketplace integrations (250+: MercadoLibre, Shopify, Amazon BR), NF-e compliance, PIX integration, 30-day trial.

**Threat level: VERY LOW** (different language, e-commerce focused). But playbook of "deep vertical integrations + affordable pricing + tax compliance" is worth studying.

### 5. Frontino (Venezuela) — Local Competitor

- **Funding:** $0 (bootstrapped, Carrd landing)

**Weaknesses:** Zero users, zero traction, no Capterra/G2/Crunchbase, single fake testimonial, Carrd landing, no analytics.

## Market Data

### TAM/SAM/SOM Calculation (LATAM example)

**TAM (Total Addressable):** All SMBs in target countries that could use BI software
- 4-country total: ~17.5M businesses
- Average $29/mo: ~$6.1B annual TAM

**SAM (Serviceable Available):** Formal SMBs in retail/restaurants/pharmacies/hardware with 2+ employees, $5K+/mo revenue, tech-ready
- 5-10% of TAM: ~1M-1.75M businesses
- At $29/mo: $348M-$609M annual SAM

**SOM (Serviceable Obtainable, Year 1):** Realistic capture in first 12 months
- Target: 200 paying customers at $39 average MRR
- Annual: $93,600 ARR
- 0.001% of SAM — highly achievable

### Willingness to Pay (LATAM benchmarks)

- Alegra: $17-$67/mo in Colombia
- Siigo: $35-$50/mo in Colombia
- Bling: $10-$120/mo in Brazil
- Frontino: $100-$250/mo in Venezuela (but 0 users)
- Toast (restaurant POS): $69/mo + payment processing
- A 7-location Venezuelan supermarket chain did not flinch at $90/mo founder deal

**Your $19-$149/mo range is competitive.**

### Framework 2: Build vs. Buy vs. Partner

When evaluating feature requests:

1. **Strengthens your moat?** → BUILD (priority)
2. **Table-stakes for the vertical?** → BUILD (necessary)
3. **Partner already does this well?** → INTEGRATE (API/webhook)
4. **One-time compliance requirement?** → OUTSOURCE

Examples:
- Customer analytics (RFM) → BUILD (moat)
- Electronic invoicing → INTEGRATE with Alegra/Siigo (partnership opportunity)
- Payment processing → INTEGRATE (Stripe, Lemon Squeezy)
- POS hardware → PARTNER

### Framework 3: Market Entry Sequencing (LATAM example)

**Phase 1 (Months 1-3): Home market + early adopters** — low competition in your niche, founder network.

**Phase 2 (Months 4-6): Adjacent market with shared language** — most advanced SaaS adoption.

**Phase 3 (Months 7-9): Largest Spanish-speaking SaaS market** — Colombia. Reframe as "intelligence upgrade from spreadsheets."

**Phase 4 (Months 10-12): Largest absolute market** — Mexico. Requires SAT compliance investment.

### Framework 4: Pricing Competitive Response

| Scenario | Response |
|----------|----------|
| Incumbent adds basic analytics | Emphasize depth (11 RFM segments vs their 3 reports). Publish comparison. |
| Low-traction local competitor gets traction | Accelerate marketing in that market. |
| New VC-funded competitor | Focus on profitability and retention, not fundraising. Bootstrapped = sustainable. |
| Big player acquires analytics company | Bet integration takes 12+ months. Move fast on acquisition. |
| Race to bottom on price | Never compete on price. Compete on insight quality and time-to-value. |

## <TU_PRODUCTO>-Specific Recommendations

### 1. POSITIONING: Define the analytics layer nobody else fills

Every competitor sells "manage your business." You sell "understand your business" (who are your best customers, who is about to leave, what will you sell next month, which products make you money).

**Tagline options to test:**
- "Tus datos ya existen. Las respuestas, también." (Your data already exists. So do the answers.)
- "No es otro sistema de ventas. Es el cerebro de tu negocio."

### 2. DISTRIBUTION: WhatsApp-First, Accountant-Partnered (LATAM specific)

What works in LATAM SMB SaaS:
- WhatsApp is the #1 business comm channel. 75% who message a business end up buying.
- Accountants are trusted advisors. Alegra and Siigo leverage accountant networks.
- Relationship-driven sales culture — cold digital ads alone fail.

**Channel priority:**
1. WhatsApp outreach
2. **Accountant partnership** — offer accountants a free dashboard for each client they onboard. Accountants become the sales channel.
3. Instagram/TikTok vertical content
4. Google SEO (vertical landing pages)
5. Referral program

### 3. PRICING: Validate, Don't Change

Current pricing is competitive vs all competitors. The problem is not price — it's awareness and trust. Invest in distribution, not discounts.

### 4. FEATURE PRIORITIES (Competitive Response)

**Next priorities:**
1. **Real customer testimonials** — worth 10x any feature
2. **Electronic invoicing integration** — table stakes in Colombia/Mexico (partner with Alegra API)
3. **WhatsApp notifications** — "5 customers at churn risk this week" via WhatsApp
4. **Daily exchange rate sync** (Frontino has this, you may need it for dual-currency markets)
5. **Marketplace integrations** — MercadoLibre, Shopify (Bling's playbook)

### 5. COMPETITIVE MONITORING

| Competitor | Signal | Action |
|-----------|--------|--------|
| Alegra | Launches "analytics" module | Publish comparison showing 10x depth |
| Siigo | Acquires analytics company | Accelerate customer acquisition |
| Treinta | Returns focus to SMB tools | Monitor strategy, don't copy free tier |
| Frontino | First 100 users | Increase regional marketing spend |
| New VC-funded entrant | — | Evaluate approach, double down on vertical depth |

### 6. THE MOAT

1. **Analytics depth** — modules no competitor has
2. **Vertical flexibility** — serves multiple verticals with same platform
3. **Dual-currency native** — built for VE economic reality, works globally
4. **Bootstrap economics** — no VC pressure; focus on retention and profitability
5. **Founder proximity to market** — in the market, talks to customers daily
6. **First-mover** — 12-18 month window before someone copies

## Appendix: Why SMB SaaS Fails in LATAM (Pitfalls to Avoid)

1. **Customer switching inertia** — "If it ain't broke, don't fix it." Must demonstrate clear immediate ROI.
2. **Non-confrontational feedback culture** — Customers won't tell you "no". Must actively extract honest feedback.
3. **Relationship-driven sales** — Cold outreach fails. Need warm intros, local references.
4. **Excessive optimism** — Founders overestimate growth. Practical pessimism wins.
5. **Free-tier trap** — Massive free bases that never convert (Treinta's lesson). Charge from day one.
6. **Ignoring local tax compliance** — DIAN, SAT are table stakes.
7. **Copying US playbooks** — LATAM is 5-10 years behind. What works in US often doesn't translate.

## Distribution Channels That Work in LATAM SMB SaaS

1. **WhatsApp** — 75% purchase conversion when customers message businesses
2. **Accountant/contador partnerships** — Trusted advisors, natural distribution
3. **Instagram/TikTok short-form** — "Business tip" format drives awareness
4. **Google SEO** — Vertical landing pages ranking for "[vertical] + software"
5. **Referral programs** — Word-of-mouth is king
6. **Local reseller networks**
7. **Marketplace integrations** — Inside MercadoLibre/Shopify drives organic discovery
8. **Industry associations** — Chambers, gremios, trade groups
